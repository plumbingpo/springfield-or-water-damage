A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Springfield Restoration Crew provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Springfield, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle sewage cleanup from start to finish:

Hazard Check and Containment

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs
  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared
  • Do not wait on a slow drain, which can turn into a full blockage
  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.
